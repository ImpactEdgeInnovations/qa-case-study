Integration testing: mobile channel to backend service

When a customer taps Send, the app asks a backend service to process it. These tests check that the app and the backend agree on what happened.

In simple words

Think of a restaurant. The app is the waiter and the backend is the kitchen. The waiter takes your order to the kitchen (the request), and the kitchen answers (the response). Integration testing checks that the two agree: right order, right amount, and the bill matches what the kitchen made.

What goes wrong in a bank: the kitchen says "we can't make it" but the waiter still charges you, or the kitchen never answers and the waiter charges you anyway. I test those cases.

Note: the failures in this demo (server error, no response, slow) are simulated with the dropdown. A real failure would be caused by the network or the server, but the app's behaviour is what I am testing.

What is integrated

Mobile app (app.html) sends the transfer details to the backend service (a free practice service), which replies with a result code and the details it received.

The app creates a reference such as TXN-123456 and sends it with the recipient and amount. A correct integration means the reply sends back the same reference and details, and the app's screen, balance and history match the reply.

Limit: the practice service does not keep any data, so I check the details it sends back, not a saved record. To trigger failures, open the app, go to Send money and use the "Backend condition" dropdown. The "What was sent and received" box under the button shows exactly that.

Sample failure investigation: DEF-07

Report: a customer taps Send, sees "Something went wrong", and later notices the money has left their balance.

  1. Reproduce: open app v1.0, set the condition to "No response", send KES 1,000. The balance drops and the message is vague.
  2. Capture evidence: the "What was sent and received" box shows something sent but no reply. Screenshot it.
  3. Find the layer: the screen showed an error (screen layer fine), the backend gave no reply (backend or network layer), and the app deducted money without a confirmed reply (app logic layer). The defect is in the app logic.
  4. Compare the data: the request had a reference and amount, but no reply came, so the app should not treat the transfer as done.
  5. Escalate with: reference, time, steps, screenshots, expected behaviour (mark as pending, do not debit, show the reference) and severity High, since customer money is affected.
  6. Verify the fix: in v1.1 the balance is unchanged, history shows "Transfer (pending)" with the reference, and the message tells the customer not to retry.
